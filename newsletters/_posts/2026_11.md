# Project highlight


<p align="center">
  <img src="https://biifsweden.github.io/images/projects/2025-09-18-UlfLandegren2025-8314.jpg" width="500" alt="Project image">
</p>

Oral cancer is becoming more common worldwide, and brushing a few thousand cells from a suspicious area of the mouth with a cytobrush offers a cheap, non-invasive way to monitor patients repeatedly. The cells are examined both for their morphology, using standard Papanicolaou staining, and at the protein level, using antibody staining with in situ proximity ligation assay (isPLA). **Christophe Avenel** and **Suganya Sivagurunathan** are developing a pipeline that quantifies the number of isPLA signals and signal intensity in single cells, to help identify potentially malignant cells.

The data was provided by Caroline Dahlström and Ulf Landegren from Uppsala University.

# Publication highlight 

<p align="center">

Image from Mardamshina *et al*, 2026 (CC BY)
 
 **Christophe Avenel** and **Carolina Wählby** are co-authors of a paper on multiplexed Deep Visual Proteomics (mxDVP) in human pancreatic islets. The paper introduces PIPΣX (Pipeline for Image Processing and Exploration), a Python tool with a graphical interface that handles the whole image analysis workflow in one place: preprocessing, segmentation, quantification, clustering and visualization. It works with TissUUmaps for interactive exploration of the results.

Read the full publication here: [Mariya Mardamshina, Nicolai Dorka, Marvin Thielert, Frida Björklund, Frederic Ballllosera Navarro, Anna Martinez Casals, Ferenc Kovacs, **Christophe Avenel**, Jocelyn E Manning Fox, Peter Horvath, **Carolina Wählby**, Patrick E MacDonald, Matthias Mann, Emma Lundberg. # Calling all bioimage computing experts in Sweden

A grassroots network is taking shape for people in Sweden who work hands-on on the computational side of bioimaging. That includes image analysts, data stewards, IT infrastructure specialists, tool developers and research software engineers. Run by and for these experts, the network would be a place to catch up regularly with peers, share know-how, and coordinate informally across organizational boundaries. Jonas Windhager and Rafael Camacho started the conversation, but the format, scope and name will be decided together with the people who join. If this describes your work, please get in touch with Jonas (jonas.windhager@scilifelab.se) or Rafael (rafael.camacho@gu.se). A first 1-hour online call is planned before the winter break.

# Swedish Bioinformatics Advisory Program

NBIS/SciLifeLab offers PhD students a senior bioinformatician as a personal advisor for up to two years.
PhD students in biological or medical research at any Swedish university can apply, preferably with at least two years left before their dissertation, and the application needs approval from both the student and their supervisor.
Apply by Monday **21 December 2026** with a short description of your project and yourself; successful applicants will hear back by 10 February 2027. More details and the application form are on the [program page](https://www.scilifelab.se/training/the-swedish-bioinformatics-advisory-program).
